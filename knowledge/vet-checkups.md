# Routine health checks at home

tags: vet, health, checkup, weight, teeth, ears, skin, dog, cat

A weekly 10 minute nose-to-tail check catches most problems early: run hands
over the body for lumps, look in the ears for redness or smell, lift the lip to
check the gums, and part the coat to look for fleas or scabs.

Weigh the pet monthly. Cats are weighed on a bathroom scale by holding them and
subtracting the owner's weight. Sudden weight loss in a cat is one of the
earliest signs of thyroid and kidney disease.

Ear cleaning is needed every 2 to 4 weeks for floppy-eared breeds such as
cocker spaniels and basset hounds, and takes about 10 minutes. Upright-eared
breeds rarely need it.

Adult pets need an annual vet visit; senior pets (dogs over 7, cats over 10)
need one every 6 months. These are calendar appointments rather than daily
tasks and should not be added to a daily care plan.

Anything sudden — not eating for 24 hours, repeated vomiting, difficulty
urinating, or laboured breathing — is a same-day vet call, not a scheduled task.
