# Caring for senior pets

tags: senior, old, elderly, arthritis, mobility, joints, dog, cat, age

A dog is considered senior from about 7 years old (5 years for giant breeds
such as great danes and mastiffs). A cat is considered senior from about
10 years old.

Exercise must be shortened and split, not removed. A senior dog that used to
take one 45 minute walk should take 2 or 3 walks of 10 to 15 minutes instead.
Long single sessions aggravate arthritis and stiff joints. Never schedule a
walk longer than 20 minutes for a senior dog with mobility problems.

Senior cats groom themselves less effectively because reaching is painful, so
they need assisted brushing of about 10 minutes 2 to 3 times per week even if
they never needed it before.

Senior pets need more frequent litter box or potty access because bladder
control declines. Add an extra 5 minute potty break to the daily routine.

Joint supplements and medications are usually given with food, so a senior pet
on medication should have the medication task placed immediately after a meal.
