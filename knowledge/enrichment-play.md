# Enrichment and play

tags: play, enrichment, toys, boredom, mental, training, dog, cat

Enrichment means mental work rather than physical exercise: puzzle feeders,
scent games, training sessions and wand play. It prevents the boredom
behaviours owners usually mistake for disobedience.

Dogs need about 15 to 20 minutes of enrichment per day. A puzzle feeder at
mealtime counts and adds no extra time to the schedule, which makes it the best
option for a tight time budget.

Cats need 2 or 3 interactive play sessions of 10 to 15 minutes each. Cats hunt
at dawn and dusk, so morning and evening sessions match their natural rhythm
and reduce night-time waking.

Enrichment is medium priority. It ranks below feeding, medication and walks,
but above bathing and nail trims, because the behavioural cost of skipping it
appears within days.

Ten focused minutes beats thirty distracted ones. A shorter session that the
owner will actually do is better than a long one that gets skipped.
